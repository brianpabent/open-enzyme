# The most curious thread is the GSDMD pore self-delivery paradox (bioRxiv Feb 2025).

The most curious thread is the **GSDMD pore self-delivery paradox** (bioRxiv Feb 2025). Corpus evidence supporting the hunch is the February 2025 preprint demonstrating that GSDMD pores (10–20 nm) serve as passive delivery conduits for membrane-impermeant caspase inhibitors and GSDMD-blocking peptides (Ac-FLTD-CMK, Z-YVAD-FMK) into pyroptotic cells — exactly the cells that are releasing IL-1β during a gout flare. The specific evidence that would refute it is a human synovial-fluid experiment showing that membrane-impermeant caspase-1 inhibitors do *not* enter GSDMD-pore-expressing macrophages faster than control cells during MSU stimulation (or that pore lifetime is too short for meaningful drug influx). The cheapest experiment that would discriminate is a THP-1 MSU-stimulation assay with fluorescently-labeled membrane-impermeant caspase inhibitor ± GSDMD-pore induction (use a low-dose nigericin trigger to control pore formation); measure intracellular fluorescence by flow cytometry or confocal microscopy. This is idiosyncratic taste — the synthesis daemon has not yet surfaced this paradox in prior passes, and it sits at the intersection of CP4 and CP6b where the platform currently has no coverage. (source: gsdmd-pore-delivery-paradox.md; nlrp3-exploit-map.md §CP4/CP6b)
