# Does theaflavins + EGCG achieve additive CP1a + CP2/CP3 + URAT1 coverage without exceeding the hepatotoxicity ceiling?

5. **Does theaflavins + EGCG achieve additive CP1a + CP2/CP3 + URAT1 coverage without exceeding the hepatotoxicity ceiling?** Theaflavins are mechanism-orthogonal to EGCG at the NLRP3 assembly step (NLRP3-NEK7 disruption vs. proteasome-mediated IκB stabilization) and provide unique URAT1/GLUT9 downregulation + OAT1/OCTN1/OAT2 upregulation (the only multi-transporter renal urate handling profile in the stack besides carnosine). The net effect on the gut-lumen sink is favorable (up-regulator, not inhibitor), removing theaflavins from the ABCG2-inhibitor warning table and making EGCG + theaflavins a recommended pairing. However, both compounds carry a hepatotoxicity signal at high doses (EGCG ~86 nM 20S proteasome IC50; theaflavins have a documented ALT/AST elevation signal above 1,000 mg/day chronic). The dose-and-chronicity axis is the load-bearing variable — dietary tea is plausibly favorable, mega-dose extract is plausibly the opposite. A small n=1 pilot (black-tea theaflavin-enriched extract 200 mg/day + matcha 400 mg EGCG/day) with monthly serum UA and ALT/AST would test the additive prediction and the hepatotoxicity ceiling in a real-world dietary context. (Speculative — the combination is not tested; the hepatotoxicity ceiling is documented for EGCG but unquantified for theaflavins at supplement doses.)
