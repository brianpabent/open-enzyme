1. **Theaflavins are ABCG2 substrates and net-favorable up-regulators in vivo, not functional inhibitors, removing them from the ABCG2-inhibitor warning table and making EGCG + theaflavins a recommended additive pairing.** Locations: theaflavins.md §2, abcg2-modulators.md §"The supplements-stack contradiction" + §"EGCG paradox is not a one-off — it is a candidate tea-polyphenol class pattern", supplements-stack.md §"Stack-level contradictions", nlrp3-inhibitor-screen.md (2026-05-05 audit adding theaflavins as Tier 2). Analysis: The 2026-05-05 audit surfaced theaflavins as a Tier 2 candidate with direct MSU peritonitis Animal Model (Chen 2023 PMID 37221235) and multi-transporter URAT1/GLUT9/OAT modulation distinct from EGCG. The multilingual lit scan 2026-06-01 (English + Chinese CNKI/WanFang sources, 茶黄素 × ABCG2/尿酸转运体) found no transporter-inhibition assay and no ChEMBL bioactivity record. Tai 2020 (*J Funct Foods* 66:103803) shows theaflavins up-regulate ABCG2 at the gene level in hyperuricemic mice while lowering serum urate (attributed to Nrf2/HO-1). This is the opposite direction to the in vitro inhibition story for EGCG/quercetin. The unifying hypothesis is hormetic, Nrf2-driven transcriptional up-regulation under chronic exposure — the same mechanism by which sulforaphane works. This places EGCG and theaflavins provisionally in the Nrf2-inducer bucket rather than the net-inhibitor bucket for chronic dietary exposure. The in vivo data are rodent and transcript-level, the Nrf2 mechanism is inferred, and the acute inhibition is real at high concentrated-extract doses (hence the high-dose stratification rows). The dose-and-chronicity axis is the load-bearing variable — dietary tea plausibly favorable, mega-dose extract plausibly the opposite. This remains a hypothesis, not a resolution.
