# Independent comp review — comp-004

## Bottom-line verdict
Action required — the arithmetic is internally reproducible, but the artifact overstates what the model resolves. It supports a **nominal gut-lumen IC50-occupancy hazard screen** for quercetin and curcumin, not a validated quantitative prediction of urate-specific intestinal ABCG2 inhibition. Key source values are citation-string/provenance-level only, sensitivity analysis is absent, and several corpus surfaces need reconciliation with later per-compound caveats, especially EGCG and quercetin.

## Implementation and constraint closure
I traced `inputs/compounds.json`, `inputs/gut_model.json`, and `inputs/ic50_data.json` through `analyze.py` into `outputs/risk_assessment.json` and `outputs/summary.md`.

**Arithmetic / code path**
- Gut mass = `dose_mg × (1 − bioavailability_fraction)`.
- Dose-limited concentration = gut mass / `small_intestine_lumen_volume_L`.
- Effective dissolved concentration = `min(dose_limited_mg_L, intestinal_solubility_mg_L)`.
- nM conversion = `mg/L × 1e6 / MW`.
- Risk ratio = effective dissolved nM / `ic50_nM`, only when `mechanism == "direct_transport_inhibition"`.
- Predicted inhibition = `ratio / (ratio + 1)`, Hill coefficient fixed at 1.
- Quercetin and curcumin arithmetic matches committed outputs:
  - Quercetin: 15 mg/L / 302.24 g/mol = 49.6 µM; 49,629 nM / 7,250 nM = 6.85; inhibition 87.3%.
  - Curcumin: 5 mg/L / 368.38 g/mol = 13.6 µM; 13,573 nM / 1,630 nM = 8.33; inhibition 89.3%.
- EGCG is computed at 500 mg/L = 1,090.8 µM but is not scored because `ic50_nM` is null and mechanism is expression regulation.

**Stored-but-unused / weakly used inputs**
- `aqueous_solubility_mg_L` is not used; only `intestinal_solubility_mg_L` drives results.
- `ph_range`, `ph_source`, `transit_time_hours`, `transit_note`, and `abcg2_expression_note` are passed through to output but do not affect concentration, residence time, or exposure.
- `assay_applicable`, `cell_line`, `ic50_values_nM`, `ic50_used_nM`, `ic50_derivation`, fetch metadata, and several target fields are documentation/output only; not used for logic.
- `bioavailability_source` and solubility notes are documentation only.
- This is mostly acceptable if declared as a simple screen, but not if the result is interpreted as a physiologic urate-flux prediction.

**Question/model fit**
- The stated question asks whether supplements reach gut-lumen concentrations sufficient to inhibit **ABCG2-mediated urate efflux**.
- The implemented model instead asks whether a **nominal solubility-capped total luminal parent-compound concentration** exceeds an **ABCG2 IC50 from non-urate, cancer-cell efflux assays**.
- Hidden substitutions:
  1. Total dissolved concentration substitutes for free monomeric inhibitor at the enterocyte apical membrane.
  2. Hoechst/mitoxantrone efflux IC50 substitutes for urate-transport inhibition constant.
  3. MCF7 / MCF7-VP overexpression systems substitute for polarized enterocytes.
  4. Static whole-small-intestine concentration substitutes for segment-resolved, time-resolved exposure.
  5. Hill n=1 occupancy substitutes for actual ABCG2 inhibition curve and substrate competition.
- Therefore the computation is a **hazard-prior screen**, not a resolved answer to physiologic sufficiency.

**Constraint closure**
- **Transport reaction closure:** ABCG2 is ATP-driven urate efflux; the model does not include urate concentration, ATP/Mg²⁺ availability, basolateral uptake, apical transporter density, transporter turnover, or competing substrates.
- **Operating constants:** IC50 values are present for quercetin and curcumin, but not urate-specific and not enterocyte-specific. No Km/Ki/Kd/IC50 comparison is made for urate itself or for luminal free inhibitor.
- **Mass balance:** Oral supplement mass and dissolved solubility cap are finite, but undissolved reservoir/dissolution kinetics, degradation, binding to bile/micelles/food, and luminal replenishment during transit are not modeled.
- **Time / residence:** `transit_time_hours` is stored but unused. The model cannot distinguish a brief proximal peak from sustained inhibition over the relevant urate-efflux window.
- **Localization / access:** Uses whole-small-intestine volume and does not resolve duodenum/jejunum vs ileum/colon, mucus layer access, unstirred water layer, micellar sequestration, or apical-membrane free concentration.
- **Coproducts / off-targets / safety:** Not modeled. For this experiment that is partly acceptable because the endpoint is transporter antagonism, but the corpus-level “risk” framing invites clinical/supplement interpretation; safety and off-targets should remain explicitly out of scope.
- **Sensitivity ranges:** No implemented sensitivity analysis. Dominant uncertainties — intestinal solubility, free fraction, segmental volume, IC50 translation, quercetin aggregation, curcumin formulation, gut metabolic stability, and urate-specific inhibition — are only discussed narratively.

**Specific inconsistency**
- `compounds.json` says EGCG’s “dose-limiting factor is bioavailability, not solubility,” but the implemented model and outputs mark EGCG as solubility-capped because dose-limited 1,568 mg/L exceeds the 500 mg/L solubility value. EGCG is not scored, so this does not change the quercetin/curcumin verdict, but the input note and summary wording need reconciliation.

## New connections or implications
- The artifact’s key useful output is not “avoid these compounds” but **which wet-lab concentrations should be benchmarked**: quercetin around 50 µM and curcumin around 10–20 µM are rational Caco-2 urate-flux challenge concentrations, provided free concentration and cytotoxicity are measured.
- Curcumin is the most robust strategic warning in the corpus because later `abcg2-modulators.md` notes in-vivo primate BCRP inhibition for drug substrates; comp-004’s curcumin result aligns with that direction, though still not urate-specific.
- Quercetin’s comp-004 result should be linked to a **proximal-small-intestine acute exposure hypothesis**, not whole-gut sustained inhibition, because later corpus text flags degradation/segment limits and quercetin aggregation.
- EGCG should be separated completely from the quercetin/curcumin acute-occupancy framework. The artifact already sets `NOT_APPLICABLE`; downstream pages should stop treating comp-004 as evidence that EGCG is an acute ABCG2 antagonist.
- The model’s solubility cap makes dose and bioavailability largely irrelevant for quercetin/curcumin once above saturation; the dominant experimental need is therefore not dose escalation but free-luminal/enterocyte-accessible concentration measurement under realistic formulation and food-matrix conditions.
