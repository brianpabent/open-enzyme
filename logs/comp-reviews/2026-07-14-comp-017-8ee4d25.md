# Independent comp review — comp-017

## Bottom-line verdict

Action required — comp-017 is a useful qualitative literature-reframing artifact, but it does **not quantitatively resolve** the stated GTEx/HPA human intestinal sex-stratification question. The main verdict (“null or near-null healthy-baseline sex dimorphism”) is plausible as a provisional synthesis, but it rests on snippet-tier secondary/animal/cell-line evidence rather than direct GTEx/HPA primary numerical extraction or line-anchored full-text verification. There is also a concrete code/input schema bug that drops the Hoque abstract-vs-full-text correction from `outputs/results.json` / `outputs/summary.md`.

## Implementation and constraint closure

**What the code actually does**

- `analyze.py` is a deterministic stdlib-only JSON aggregator. It reads:
  - `inputs/gtex_data.json`
  - `inputs/hpa_data.json`
  - `inputs/full_text_extract.json`
- It does **not** fetch GTEx/HPA, parse primary papers, perform statistics, compute fold-changes, or verify citations.
- The overall verdict is effectively **hard-coded** in `assemble_overall_verdict()`. The small keyword counts in `aggregate_part_a()` do not drive the verdict.
- Many “unused” heuristic leaves are documentation/provenance fields rather than expected computational inputs. However, some load-bearing interpretive fields are not propagated to output.

**Concrete implementation defect**

- `aggregate_part_b()` reads `abstract_vs_fulltext_difference` only from inside `p["full_text_extract"]`:

  ```python
  p.get("full_text_extract", {}).get("abstract_vs_fulltext_difference")
  ```

- In `inputs/full_text_extract.json`, Hoque/P01 stores `abstract_vs_fulltext_difference` at the **paper top level**, not inside `full_text_extract`.
- Result: `outputs/results.json` has:

  ```json
  "abstract_vs_fulltext_difference": null
  ```

  for P01, and `outputs/summary.md` leaves Hoque’s “Abstract-vs-fulltext difference” section blank.
- This is exactly the experiment’s highest-value correction versus comp-016: 88% combined intestinal measurement vs 78% Western jejunum, and 78%:44% as the cleaner Western:Western contrast. The README/wiki archive mention it, but the machine-readable output loses it.

**Question/model fit**

- Part A asks whether **healthy human male/female intestinal ABCG2 expression distributions** differ meaningfully at population scale.
- The artifact does **not** contain direct GTEx per-tissue sex-stratified TPM/log2FC/FDR values, nor HPA sex-stratified intestinal protein data.
- The Part A implementation therefore cannot answer the stated human public-database question quantitatively. It can only say: “direct database access failed; fallback literature did not surface strong evidence of healthy-baseline intestinal sex dimorphism.”
- The conclusion substitutes:
  - rat intestinal baseline null,
  - human hepatic ABCG2 protein null,
  - secondary reviews / GWAS sex-association framing,
  - disease-state Q140K mouse dimorphism,
  - supraphysiological Caco-2 hormone perturbations,
  for direct healthy-human intestinal population distributions.
- That substitution is disclosed in limitations, but some summary language still reads stronger than the implemented evidence tier warrants.

**Constraint closure**

- This is not an enzyme-capacity or mass-balance model. No reaction stoichiometry, urate flux balance, ATP cost, transporter saturation, Km/Kd, luminal residence time, or serum-urate mapping is implemented.
- Relevant biological closure status:
  - **ABCG2 substrate/product context:** urate efflux via ATP-dependent apical transporter is the biological target, but no transporter kinetics or flux model is implemented.
  - **Operating concentrations:** comp-017 correctly flags Yu 2021 Caco-2 EB at 100 µM as 5–6 orders above physiological serum E2, and Klyushova 1–100 µM sex-hormone treatments as supraphysiological or pharmacological-sensor tier.
  - **Localization:** evidence spans Caco-2, mouse jejunum/kidney, Wistar rat intestine, human liver protein, and secondary GTEx/HPA literature. Human healthy intestinal tissue remains unresolved directly.
  - **Time base:** 24–48 h cell exposure and disease-state animal phenotypes are not equivalent to lifelong healthy baseline sex differences.
  - **Disease vs baseline:** Hoque Q140K/Q141K is disease/genetic stress evidence, not healthy-baseline expression evidence.
  - **Sensitivity:** no computational sensitivity analysis. Dominant uncertainties are not parameter-swept; they are qualitatively listed: direct GTEx/HPA access, primary line-anchored verification, physiological hormone response magnitude, species translation, clomiphene intestinal ER pharmacology.
  - **Safety/off-targets:** not directly modeled. Claims about stack-design implications are mechanistic research hypotheses, not clinical guidance.

## New connections or implications

- comp-017 is stronger as a **negative-mechanism filter** than as a positive H07 confirmation. It substantially weakens “AR-mediated intestinal ABCG2 suppression,” but leaves the core clomiphene intestinal ER agonist/antagonist question unresolved.
- The validation design should pivot from “DHT suppression” to a more neutral factorial: physiological vs supraphysiological androgen, E2, TNFα, PXR/FXR inhibitors, ABCG2 total/surface protein, and urate flux. This would directly test the comp-017 reframe.
- The Hosoyamada/Smct1 finding affects more than H07: any corpus statement summarizing the androgen renal mechanism as “T → URAT1 protein ↑” should be revised to “Smct1 protein ↑ + GLUT9 attenuation + URAT1 mRNA ↑, with URAT1 protein unchanged at the relevant baseline tier,” pending primary line verification.
- HPA interindividual variability should not be used as a statistical argument that sex effects are “swamped.” It is a power/design issue, not proof of null mean difference.
