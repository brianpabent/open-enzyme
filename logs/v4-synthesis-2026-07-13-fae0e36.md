## Contradictions Found

1. **The "chassis-as-formulation" argument for H₂O₂ housekeeping is now quantitatively resolved across all three intra-articular uricase architectures, but it is driven by total catalase capacity at the joint scale, not by residue-level proximity.** Locations: `chassis-pending-interventions.md` §6, `delivery-route-matrix.md` §"Why SC uricase doesn't work", `intra-articular-uricase-h2o2-reaction-diffusion-computational.md` (comp-035), `engineered-koji-protocol.md` §"The Hydrogen Peroxide Question". Analysis: The Liu 2025 Pickering emulsion paper asserts FRET-confirmed <10 nm proximity as the safety mechanism. comp-035's Damköhler-number analysis shows Da_shell ~5×10^{-3} (median), escape fraction ~0.998 — the catalase shell is too thin to scavenge H₂O₂ before it diffuses to the bulk. Safety is driven by bulk-phase catalase scavenging from all dispersed droplets in the joint volume (mathematically equivalent to free co-formulated catalase at the same total dose). The architecture-distinguishing edge case is the free co-formulated YELLOW outcome at uneven URI:CAT ratio (31.6 µM H₂O₂). Catalase (kcat/Km) is the dominant sensitivity driver (Spearman r = −0.95 to −0.97). This reframes the qualitative "H₂O₂ diffuses faster than catalase can scavenge it" critique in `delivery-route-matrix.md` as correct for SC depots but not for IA at typical dosing. The chassis-as-formulation argument for oral whole-cell uricase (peroxisomal co-localization) is now quantitatively corroborated by the IA bulk-catalase scavenging result. The two routes solve the same biochemistry problem through different mechanisms; both depend on the same load-bearing variable (total catalase capacity at the reaction site). The previous narrative that "the chassis solves it for free" is technically correct for the oral whole-cell case but is now qualified by the quantitative analysis — intracellular catalase is directly co-localized only with intracellular uricase; secreted or surface-displayed uricase generates peroxide outside that compartment. (Mechanistic Extrapolation, in silico over published In Vitro kinetic anchors.)

Cheapest discriminating experiment: $500 spike/recovery study on sodium-butyrate standards in sterile and spent medium, measured in parallel by HPLC-UV and GC-MS (contract lab or community-biolab with both instruments). I suspect another sweep model would converge on this pick (it's the obvious next step from comp-038's YELLOW verdict and the workflow's explicit butyrate-specific limitation), so this is likely convergent rather than idiosyncratic.
