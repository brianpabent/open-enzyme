The corpus evidence: comp-023 (FBA on iWV1314) returned GREEN at the Jeennor 2023 empirical titer (564 mg/L/day) for cns1+cns2 cordycepin, with cordycepin demand consuming ~0.02% of cellular carbon flux at that titer; the breakpoint is ~1,000× the empirical titer (~564 g/L/d). The chaperone-orthogonal-stacking framework §5.6 explicitly states that cytosolic payloads (CarnS + panD for carnosine, native ergothioneine biosynthesis) impose zero PDI/ERO1 load and do not compete for chaperone capacity — the practical stacking limit for the endgame strain is the count of *secreted* cassettes, not total cassette count. The evidence that would refute it is a measured cytosolic-cassette burden >5% of total proteome cost in a proteome-constrained model (pcSecKoji) or a growth-rate penalty >10% in a multi-cassette strain (uricase + Lf + cytosolic payload). The cheapest discriminating experiment is to run the §1.9 dual-cassette (uricase + Lf) experiment with a parallel triple-cassette arm that adds the carnosine module (CarnS + panD, cytosolic, zero disulfides) and measure growth rate, total secreted protein, and per-cassette titers (uricase activity, Lf ELISA, carnosine LC-MS). Marginal cost ~$1–2K on existing infrastructure; the same fermentation batch can run both arms. This is idiosyncratic — most models would default to the secreted DAF SCR1-4 arm because it closes CP0; the cytosolic escape is the thread that keeps the single-strain endgame thesis alive even if PDI capacity is the binding constraint.
