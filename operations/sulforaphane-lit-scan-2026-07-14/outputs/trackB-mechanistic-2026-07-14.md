# Sulforaphane (SFN) — Track B: Western-language mechanistic refresh (post-2022)

**Date:** 2026-07-14
**Scope:** Western-indexed (PubMed/PMC + ChEMBL) mechanistic literature, 2022–2026, on the three SFN mechanisms Open Enzyme already tracks (Nrf2→NF-κB priming suppression; Nrf2-independent direct inflammasome inhibition; Nrf2→ABCG2 gut-sink induction + Q141K), plus XO-direct, HDAC/epigenetic, and bioavailability/formulation.
**Attribution:** All primary literature below retrieved via PubMed; DOIs linked inline. ChEMBL v34 for kinetics.
**Bottom line:** No paradigm-shifting new mechanism since 2022. The refresh (a) *sharpens* the priming axis (SFN metabolites carry NLRP3/TLR4-NF-κB suppression; only parent SFN moves Nrf2), (b) *confirms* SFN as a bona-fide food-grade HDAC inhibitor **as a mechanism class** but leaves the corpus's SFN→Q141K-rescue leg unsupported, (c) surfaces a **contradictory** ABCG2 signal (SFN is a PXR antagonist that can *lower* ABCG2 via the PXR arm), and (d) finds **no** direct xanthine-oxidase inhibition data. Bioavailability/formulation is where the genuinely new quantitative data lives.

## New mechanistic findings since 2022

### 1. SFN metabolites also suppress NLRP3 — but via TLR4/NF-κB, not Nrf2 (sharpens mechanism #1)
**Pagliarani et al. 2026, *Pharmacol Rep* 78(2):615–624** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43440-026-00823-z), PMID 41572056). **In Vitro** (human TLR4-reporter HEK293 + THP-1 macrophages, LPS-primed).
- Parent SFN **and** its circulating metabolites (SFN-glutathione, SFN-cysteine, SFN-N-acetylcysteine) each decreased TLR4 activation, NLRP3 protein, nuclear NF-κB p65, and IL-1β/TNF-α release.
- **Key discriminator:** all four compounds disrupted the Keap1/Nrf2 complex, but **only parent SFN produced Nrf2 nuclear translocation.** The metabolites' anti-NLRP3 effect therefore runs **mainly through TLR4/NF-κB (priming)**, not the Nrf2 arm.
- *Interpretation for the corpus:* the "Nrf2 competes with NF-κB for CBP/p300 → suppresses priming" story is not the whole priming picture. A substantial share of in-vivo NLRP3 priming suppression is plausibly carried by (i) direct TLR4/NF-κB blunting and (ii) the metabolite pool (SFN-NAC/-Cys/-GSH), which persists in plasma/tissue longer than parent SFN. This is a genuine post-2022 refinement of *which* molecular species and *which* node does the priming work.

### 2. Food-grade HDAC inhibition — confirmed as a mechanism class, dose-dependent
**Somers et al. 2023, *PLoS One* 18(10):e0293075** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0293075), PMID 37856454). **Animal Model** (insect herbivores) + explicit restatement of the human/mammalian mechanism.
- Dietary SFN inhibited HDAC enzyme activity **dose-dependently**; 7-day dietary exposure reduced HDAC activity **~50%**. Paper restates the established human finding that SFN "alters enzyme activities, DNA-histone binding, and gene expression within minutes."
**Sharma & Tollefsbol 2022, *Exp Cell Res* 416(1):113160** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yexcr.2022.113160), PMID 35447103). **In Vitro** (breast cancer lines).
- SFN (with genistein + sodium butyrate) down-regulated **HDAC1, HDAC6, HDAC11** and inhibited global HDAC activity — SFN and NaB explicitly co-classed as HDAC inhibitors.
**Fawad et al. 2022, *Gastroenterology* 163(5):1377–1390** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2022.07.051), PMID 35934064). **In Vitro** (human + murine intestinal organoids/Caco-2).
- SCFAs (butyrate etc.) entrain intestinal-epithelial circadian rhythm via an HDACi-dependent mechanism; "botanical HDAC inhibitors" (SFN referenced) reproduce the effect in the same enteroid system — i.e., SFN is HDACi-active **in the intestinal epithelium**, the tissue of interest for ABCG2/Q141K.
- *Interpretation:* SFN-as-HDAC-inhibitor at food-grade exposure is well supported. **But** none of these papers demonstrate the downstream leg the corpus asserts (SFN → HDAC inhibition → Q141K ABCG2 trafficking rescue). See verification table.

### 3. Bioavailability / formulation — the richest new-data vein
**Ali Redha et al. 2025, *Food Funct* 16(1):71–86** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4fo03446e), PMID 39431890). **In Vitro** (INFOGEST 2.0 digestion + Caco-2/HT29-MTX-E12 absorption).
- **Whey-protein** microencapsulation (freeze-dried) raised SFN bioaccessibility to **67.7 ± 1.2%** (vs 19.6 ± 10.4% dried broccoli; 19.0 ± 2.2% pea-protein) and bioavailability to **54.4 ± 4.0%** (vs 15.8 ± 2.2% dried broccoli; 9.6 ± 1.2% pea-protein). Whey ≫ pea as wall material. Direct, load-bearing quantification of a stabilized-SFN formulation.
**de Vasconcelos Lopes et al. 2025, *Plant Foods Hum Nutr* 80(2):119** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11130-025-01357-0), PMID 40327164). **In Vitro** (INFOGEST + HPLC).
- SFN content **highest in 2-day-old sprouts** and decreases with germination day; SFN also **degrades/biotransforms during GI digestion** (bioaccessibility falls with sprout age). Nuance against the "older/greener sprout = more SFN" intuition; youngest sprouts carry the most SFN.
**van Steenwijk et al. 2023, *Front Nutr* 10:1204561** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2023.1204561), PMID 37485383). **Clinical Trial** (double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, n=12, NCT05146804).
- Single 16 g broccoli-sprout intake cut urinary 11-dehydro-thromboxane B2 by **50% (p=0.018)**; urinary SFN excretion correlated inversely with the thromboxane metabolite. **Pharmacogenetics:** carriers of **NQO1*2** showed **reduced SFN excretion (p=0.035)** — a responder-stratification variable directly analogous to the corpus's ABCG2-Q141K stratification logic. Human PK/PD, single-dose, fast readout.

### 4. Nrf2 as amplifier of downstream detox enzymes (context, not gout-direct)
Su & Penning 2023/2025 (*Chem Res Toxicol*, [DOI 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.2c00337) PMID 36693016; [DOI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrestox.5c00101) PMID 40633532) confirm l-SFN acts as a functional NRF2 activator in human lung cells (inducing NRF2-dependent AKR1C/NQO1 output). **In Vitro.** Tangential to gout but reconfirms SFN's NRF2-activator identity in a human-cell system independent of the ARE-reporter assays.

## ChEMBL kinetics check (CHEMBL48802 = SULFORAPHANE)

Target CHEMBL1075094 = *Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2* (Nrf2). EC50 records, most-potent first:

|---|---|---|---|

**Verdict:** 580 nM is **verified and reproducible** in the Nrf2 nuclear-translocation (U2OS/Keap1) assay class — the corpus's "J Med Chem 2019" attribution is exactly right. But it is the potent end of a **~30× assay-dependent range** (580 nM → 19 µM). When the number is load-bearing (e.g., comparing to gut-lumen achievable concentrations), quote it as assay-specific, not as "the" SFN EC50.

Other ChEMBL functional data of note: anti-inflammatory EC50 1.78 µM in LPS-BV-2 microglia (nitrite readout, J Med Chem 2025, CHEMBL6153069) — sub-2 µM cellular anti-inflammatory potency, consistent with the priming-suppression story. Compound: max_phase 3, natural product, MW 177.29, Ro5-compliant (0 violations), QED 0.27.

## Net assessment for the corpus

1. **Add** the SFN-metabolite priming finding (Pagliarani 2026) — it reframes "which species does the priming suppression" and supports a longer-acting metabolite pool. Genuinely new.
2. **Fix** the gout-action-guide.md line-131 SFN→Q141K-rescue claim — it is an unsupported extrapolation from the butyrate/vorinostat Q141K literature; the corpus's own abcg2-modulators.md attributes Q141K rescue to non-SFN HDACi. Downgrade to Mechanistic Extrapolation and flag "SFN-specific Q141K rescue untested."
3. **Flag** the ABCG2 direction ambiguity — SFN is a PXR *antagonist* (Ho 2023) that can lower ABCG2 via the PXR arm; the Nrf2-induction direction is not the only arm. Do not present SFN→gut-ABCG2 induction as settled.
4. **Do not** assert direct XO inhibition — no evidence.
5. **Keep** the 580 nM EC50 but annotate it as assay-specific (nuclear-translocation assay; 0.6–19 µM across ARE reporters).
6. **Strongest new practical data** is formulation: whey-protein microencapsulation ~3.4× bioavailability over dried broccoli (Ali Redha 2025); youngest (2-day) sprouts carry most SFN (de Vasconcelos Lopes 2025); NQO1*2 is a human responder-stratification variant (van Steenwijk 2023).
