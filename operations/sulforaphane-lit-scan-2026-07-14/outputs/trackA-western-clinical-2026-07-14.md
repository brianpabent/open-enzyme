# Track A — Sulforaphane Western-language HUMAN & CLINICAL evidence refresh (post-2022)

**Scan date:** 2026-07-14
**Scope:** Western-language (PubMed / ClinicalTrials.gov / Consensus) human + clinical + newest animal evidence for sulforaphane (SFN) on the gout / hyperuricemia / NLRP3-inflammation axis. Baseline = corpus's newest citation Wang 2022 (*J Adv Res*, PMID 36371056).
**Tools:** bio-research MCP — PubMed (search + metadata), ClinicalTrials.gov v2, Consensus. All load-bearing numbers verified against the source record (abstract / trial protocol), not title summaries.
**Attribution:** Findings below are drawn from PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov records; DOI links included per source.

---

## NEW since the 2022 corpus (genuinely newer than Wang 2022 / Yang 2018 / Greaney 2015)

1. **Chien 2025 (Metabolites)** — FIRST human in-vivo evidence that oral glucoraphanin (→SFN) lowers **IL-1β** gene expression in tissue (skin). Randomized. **No systemic/blood effect.** No urate endpoint.
2. **van Steenwijk 2023 (Front Nutr, NCT05146804)** — human RCT; broccoli-sprout SFN produced a **mild PRO-inflammatory (hormetic)** shift under caloric challenge, not suppression. Directly complicates the "SFN suppresses systemic inflammation" narrative. No urate endpoint.
3. **Mastaloudis 2026 (Sci Rep)** — human crossover RCT: **mustard-seed myrosinase doubled SFN bioavailability** (39.8% vs 18.6%) from broccoli-seed GR. Human validation of the corpus's "add myrosinase" bioavailability hack.
4. **NCT04608903 (CKD, active/results-pending)** — human SFN trial measuring **PBMC NLRP3, IL-1β, IL-18, hs-CRP** in an inflammatory renal population; 4 g/day × 2 mo. Discovery-positive, results pending.
5. **Andrade 2026 (Pharmacol Rep, PMID 41572056)** — SFN **metabolites** (SFN-GSH/-Cys/-NAC) retain TLR4/NLRP3/IL-1β suppression in human macrophages; only parent SFN drives Nrf2 translocation. New mechanistic granularity on the Nrf2-independent arm.
6. **Zhou 2026 (Mol Nutr Food Res, PMID 42220202)** — oral SFN suppresses TLR4/NF-κB/**NLRP3/caspase-1** in vivo (mouse); reinforces oral-route inflammasome mechanism (non-gout model).
7. **Bioaccessible-SFN intestinal model (Biofactors 2026, PMID 41975658)** and **hepatocyte-pyroptosis (Front Pharmacol 2025, PMID 41181585)** — new IL-1β/IL-18/inflammasome-output mechanistic reads.

**What is NOT new:** No post-2022 SFN-specific animal hyperuricemia/gout model. No human trial with a urate, gout-flare, or ABCG2/urate-handling endpoint anywhere.

---

## HUMAN DATA STATUS — verdict

**Human SFN evidence on serum/urine urate, gout flares, or urate-handling (ABCG2/URAT1): still ZERO.** Triangulated three ways — (a) direct PubMed `sulforaphane AND urate/gout` 2022–2026 returns only the existing Wang 2022; (b) ClinicalTrials.gov `gout OR hyperuricemia OR uric acid` × `sulforaphane/broccoli/glucoraphanin` = **0 trials, total**; (c) Consensus top hits carry no urate endpoint.

**What human SFN evidence DID materialize post-2022 is on the *inflammation* axis, and it is mixed / modest:**
- Tissue-level IL-1β/TNF-α gene suppression in skin (Chien 2025) — but with an explicit **null result in blood/PBMC**, which is the compartment that matters for systemic urate-driven inflammation.
- A human RCT (van Steenwijk 2023) where SFN went the "wrong" way — a **hormetic mild pro-inflammatory** response under metabolic stress.
- A results-pending CKD trial (NCT04608903) that WILL, for the first time, report human PBMC NLRP3/IL-1β/IL-18/hs-CRP on SFN — worth tracking for readout.

**Quality of human inflammation evidence:** low. Small n (12–18), surrogate/tissue-expression or ex-vivo endpoints, no hard clinical outcome, and one directionally-contradictory RCT. **The corpus's Tier-2 supplement ranking for SFN still rests on animal (Yang 2018, Wang 2022) + in-vitro (Greaney 2015) evidence — no human urate/gout data exists to promote it further, and the new human inflammation data does not cleanly support systemic anti-inflammatory action.**

## Findings (per source)

### HUMAN / CLINICAL

#### 1. Chien 2025 — Oral glucoraphanin (+curcumin), skin cytoprotective/inflammatory genes
- **Citation:** Chien AL, Liu H, Rachidi S, … Fahey JW, Kang S. *Metabolites* 2025;15(6):360. PMID 40559384; PMC12195189. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/metabo15060360)
- **Evidence level:** Clinical Trial (randomized; mechanistic/biomarker, healthy volunteers)
- **Administered:** **Glucoraphanin (precursor)** 450 mg (1 mmol/day) — "converted to SF upon ingestion"; ± curcumin 1000 mg. Oral, 8 days. Myrosinase/gut conversion relied on endogenous conversion; SFN presence in skin confirmed in **6/12** GR-ingesting subjects.
- **Population:** n=18 healthy (9 M / 9 F), aged 18–69.
- **Key verified results:** NQO1 mRNA in skin ↑ **3.1-fold** (GR), 3.3-fold (curcumin), 3.6-fold (combo). Across all treatments (n=18), **IL-1β and TNF-α expression reduced**; IL-6, IL-17, STING, CYR61 reduced less robustly. **No significant change in blood cells (PBMC)**; erythema after UVB not significantly affected. Well tolerated.
- **Gout-axis relevance:** First human in-vivo demonstration of GR/SFN lowering IL-1β expression — but only in **skin, not blood**. The PBMC null is a direct caution against assuming systemic inflammasome suppression, which is what gout would need.

#### 2. van Steenwijk 2023 — Broccoli-sprout SFN under caloric challenge (hormesis)
- **Citation:** van Steenwijk HP, Vinken, van Osch, … de Boer A. *Frontiers in Nutrition* 2023 (7 citations). Registered **NCT05146804**. [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/93d42ee8fa8d5633b0d513737e91f634/)
- **Evidence level:** Clinical Trial (double-blind, crossover, placebo-controlled RCT)
- **Administered:** **16 g broccoli sprouts** (active SFN via intact sprout myrosinase) vs 16 g pea sprouts (placebo), single dose + standardized high-caloric drink (PhenFlex). Oral.
- **Population:** n=12 healthy.
- **Key verified results:** Broccoli-sprout SFN **increased** caloric-load-induced **CCL-2 (MCP-1)** (p=0.017); sICAM-1, sVCAM-1, hs-CRP, IL-10 showed non-significant upward tendencies; composite systemic low-grade-inflammation score trended up (p=0.087). Authors interpret as **hormetic (mild pro-inflammatory) onset**. No metabolic-stress biomarker change.
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Cautionary. A human RCT where SFN did **not** suppress systemic inflammation and mildly elevated a monocyte chemokine. Real intervention effect on systemic inflammation in humans is unproven and possibly biphasic. No urate endpoint.

#### 3. Mastaloudis 2026 — Myrosinase co-administration doubles SFN bioavailability
- **Citation:** Mastaloudis AF, et al. *Scientific Reports* 2026 (2 citations). [Consensus record](https://consensus.app/papers/details/5c19587a1b115d74bdd5394138d641dd/)
- **Evidence level:** Clinical Trial (randomized, double-blind, crossover; pharmacokinetic)
- **Administered:** Single oral dose **broccoli-seed GR + mustard-seed myrosinase** vs GR alone (both + ascorbic acid).
- **Population:** n=16 (9 F / 7 M).
- **Key verified results:** GR+Myr **doubled SFN bioavailability: 39.8 ± 3.1% vs 18.6 ± 3.1%** (urinary metabolites); first-8h conversion **25.4 ± 2.7% vs 8.0 ± 2.7%**. 4 bacterial GR-converting genes correlated with conversion (p<0.0155). No fecal community shift after single dose.
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Human validation of the corpus's "add mustard seed powder / exogenous myrosinase to restore conversion" bioavailability hack (nlrp3-exploit-map.md §bioavailability trick). Quantifies the glucoraphanin→SFN conversion problem: even with myrosinase, only ~40% bioavailable; GR-alone ~19%, highly microbiome-dependent. Load-bearing for any GR-based (vs active-SFN) delivery design.

#### 4. NCT04608903 — SFN in Chronic Kidney Disease (results pending)
- **Citation:** Universidade Federal Fluminense (Denise Mafra). ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT04608903](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04608903). Status ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING; primary completion 2023-12-31; **has_results: false**.
- **Evidence level:** Clinical Trial (registered, randomized crossover; results not posted → "discovery-positive, results pending")
- **Administered:** **4 g/day SFN** (L-sulforaphane 1%) × 2 months vs placebo (colored corn starch), crossover. Oral.
- **Population:** n=25 CKD (hemodialysis + non-dialysis), 18–75.
- **Endpoints (verified from protocol):** PBMC mRNA for Nrf2, NF-κB, NQO1, HO-1, VCAM-1, E-selectin, **NLRP3, IL-1B, IL-18**, PGC1-α; hs-CRP (ELISA); multiplex cytokines. Secondary: uremic toxins. **No uric-acid endpoint listed.**
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Highest-value pending human readout — will directly report SFN effect on human **PBMC NLRP3 / IL-1β / IL-18 / hs-CRP** in an inflamed population. Track for publication. Do NOT overstate: no results posted, no urate endpoint.

#### 5. NCT07668596 (EXSFN, UBC) — SFN + exercise immunometabolism (recruiting)
- **Citation:** University of British Columbia. ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT07668596](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07668596). RECRUITING; start 2026-03; primary completion 2027-12; no results.
- **Evidence level:** Registered trial, results pending.
- **Administered:** SFN-rich broccoli-sprout extract (BrocElite) 4 days ± HIIT, crossover. Oral.
- **Population:** n=20 healthy, 19–35.
- **Endpoints:** PBMC oxygen consumption (Seahorse); ex-vivo LPS-/PolyIC-stimulated **TNF-α**; monocyte subsets + **TLR-4 expression**; WBC. **No urate; no IL-1β/NLRP3 direct.**
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Marginal. TLR-4/monocyte focus touches the priming arm but no inflammasome-output or urate endpoint.

> **Trial-landscape note:** Broader SFN human trial registry (116 SFN/broccoli/GR trials total) is dominated by cancer chemoprevention, autism, schizophrenia, COPD, skin, and exercise. **Zero** carry a gout, hyperuricemia, or uric-acid endpoint.

### ANIMAL (newest post-2022, mechanism-adjacent — none gout-specific)

#### 6. Zhou 2026 — Oral SFN suppresses TLR4/NF-κB/NLRP3/caspase-1 in vivo
- **Citation:** Zhou G, et al. *Molecular Nutrition & Food Research* 2026;70(11):e70510. PMID 42220202; PMC13359281. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mnfr.70510)
- **Evidence level:** Animal Model (mouse, epilepsy — NOT gout)
- **Administered:** **SFN 25 mg/kg/day, oral gavage**, 7 days pretreatment.
- **Key verified results:** ↓ seizure severity, mortality, hippocampal cytokines, neuronal damage (p<0.05); ↓ TLR4, MyD88, p-NF-κB, **NLRP3, caspase-1** (p<0.05); TAK-242 (TLR4 inhibitor) added nothing beyond SFN → SFN acts through TLR4/NF-κB/NLRP3.
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Reinforces oral-route in-vivo NLRP3/caspase-1 suppression and places it upstream at TLR4/NF-κB priming (CP1/CP2 arm). Non-gout disease context.

#### 7. Andrade 2026 — SFN metabolites retain anti-NLRP3/IL-1β activity (human cells)
- **Citation:** *Pharmacological Reports* 2026. PMID 41572056. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43440-026-00823-z)
- **Evidence level:** In Vitro (human TLR4-HEK293 + THP-1 macrophages)
- **Key verified results:** SFN and metabolites (SFN-GSH, SFN-cysteine, SFN-NAC) ↓ TLR4, NLRP3, NF-κB p65, **IL-1β, TNF-α**. Only parent **SFN** drove Nrf2 nuclear translocation; metabolites act mainly via TLR4/NF-κB → distinct crosstalk.
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Refines the Nrf2-independent inflammasome arm (Greaney 2015 lineage) — circulating SFN metabolites, not just parent, carry IL-1β-suppressing activity. Human cell substrate strengthens translation relevance.

#### 8. Bioaccessible SFN — intestinal epithelium/macrophage inflammasome outputs
- **Citation:** *BioFactors* 2026. PMID 41975658. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/biof.70101)
- **Evidence level:** In Vitro (Caco-2 + THP-1 epithelium–macrophage model)
- **Key verified results:** Bioaccessible SFN **0.100 μg/mL** (physiological) ↓ IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17, **IL-18**, IL-23, TNF-α by up to **~56%**; ↑ IL-10/IL-4/IL-13 by ~24–71%; shifted macrophage CD86/CD206 toward tolerogenic.
- **Gout-axis relevance:** Gut-lumen relevance (IBD framing) — at achievable luminal concentrations SFN suppresses inflammasome outputs IL-1β/IL-18. Supports the gut-acting delivery thesis.

#### 9. Additional NLRP3-mechanism reads (supporting, non-gout)
- **Bioorg Chem 2026, PMID 41797134** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bioorg.2026.109708)): SFN as an **H₂S donor** — activates CBS-H₂S axis + mitophagy → clears damaged mitochondria → blocks mtROS-mediated **NLRP3** activation (Parkinson's mouse/BV-2). *Animal + In Vitro.* Novel upstream mechanism (mitophagy/mtROS) for NLRP3 suppression.
- **Front Pharmacol 2025, PMID 41181585** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2025.1690067)): SFN ↓ hepatocyte **pyroptosis / IL-1 / IL-18** via Nrf2-HO-1 in LPS acute liver injury (mouse, **intraperitoneal** — note non-oral route). *Animal Model.*

### NEGATIVE / NULL RESULTS (load-bearing)

- **No post-2022 SFN-specific animal hyperuricemia or gout model.** The `Brassica/broccoli × hyperuricemia × 2022–2026` PubMed query (7 hits) surfaced only NON-SFN Brassica actives — Brassica campestris pollen kaempferol (PMID 41933742), **luteolin** in HN mice (PMID 39479625; note: luteolin activates ABCG2 + Nrf2/HO-1/NQO1 + inhibits hepatic XO — mechanistically parallel to SFN's proposed axis but a different molecule), iodine-biofortified kale (PMIDs 39859542, 39519410), kohlrabi sprouts (35889759), Qiamagu juice fermentation (41185346). None test sulforaphane on urate.
- **No human urate/gout SFN trial exists** (registry total = 0; PubMed direct = Wang 2022 only).
